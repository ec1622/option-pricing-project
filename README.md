# option-pricing-project
In this project I used Monte Carlo methods to price and hedge a range of options. I began by pricing European options using both the Black-Scholes formula and Monte Carlo simulation, then priced American options using the Longstaff-Schwartz algorithm, before extending the Monte Carlo approach to Asian and knock-in barrier options. To improve the efficiency of the simulations I applied antithetic variates, control variates and importance sampling. I then computed the Greeks and used them to simulate delta hedging under proportional transaction costs. In the final section, I implemented the Whalley-Wilmott no-transaction band, which is a closed- form asymptotic approximation to the optimal hedging policy under transactional costs. I then tested numerically the cube-root scaling law between transaction cost rate and optimal band width. The objective is to demonstrate both practical implementation and theoretical understanding of numerical methods used in derivative pricing and risk management.
